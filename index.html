🌸 petal - See Exactly Where Your Disk Space Goes

A fast, exact disk-space explorer for macOS with a live sunburst chart. No guesswork, no vague estimates — just a clear picture of every folder, file, and byte.

⬇ Get petal Now

🌸 What Is petal?

petal is a desktop application that shows you where the space on your computer's drive has gone. Over months and years, drives quietly fill up with old downloads, duplicate photos, forgotten video projects, caches, and backups. Most people only notice when the drive is almost full and something stops working.

petal answers the obvious question — "What is taking up all this space?" — in a way that is easy to read at a glance. Instead of a wall of numbers, it draws a live sunburst chart: a colourful, circular map where every ring is a folder and every slice is a file or subfolder. The bigger the slice, the more space it uses. You can spot a 40 GB culprit in seconds without reading a single line of technical text.

Under the hood, petal is written in Rust and uses GPUI, a modern interface framework designed for speed. In plain terms: it opens quickly, it scans quickly, and the chart stays smooth and responsive even on folders containing hundreds of thousands of files.

It is also exact. Many tools approximate, round numbers, or skip hidden system data. petal reports real sizes so that the numbers you see are the numbers that will come back to you after you clean up.

✨ Key Features

Live sunburst chart

The centre of the window is a circular map of your drive. As petal scans, the chart fills in and updates in real time, so you can watch the picture build itself rather than staring at a progress bar.

Exact sizes, not estimates

Every number reflects the true size of your files. Nothing is rounded away, and hidden or system folders are included so the total actually matches your drive.

Built for speed

Powered by Rust, petal scans large drives far faster than typical disk tools and stays responsive while it does. You can keep resizing the window and clicking around the chart while a scan runs.

Made for macOS and APFS

petal is built specifically for modern macOS storage, including APFS drives. That means it understands the way your Mac organises data instead of treating it like a generic folder tree.

Click to drill down

Select any slice of the chart to zoom into that folder. Ring by ring, you can travel from the whole drive down to the single oversized file that is filling it up.

Nothing to configure

There is no account to create, no dataset to import, and no setup wizard. Open petal, point it at a folder or a drive, and read the chart.

Lightweight footprint

The application itself is small and stays out of the way. It does not run background agents, install helpers, or leave services running after you close the window.

Once the download has completed, open petal the same way you would open any other application on your computer. There is no separate account, licence key, or registration screen to work through.

🔍 Your First Scan

The first time petal opens, it needs to look through your folders to work out where your space has gone. This is the only slow moment you are likely to experience, and it only happens once per scan — after that, results are kept in front of you until you choose to scan again.

Pick the drive or folder you want to inspect. If you are not sure where to start, choose your main drive, because that is where almost all of your stored files live. petal will begin reading through the contents and drawing the sunburst chart as it goes.

While the scan is running you can already start using the chart. Early results show the largest, most obvious folders first, which is usually exactly what you want to know. You do not have to wait for a complete scan before learning something useful.

When the scan finishes, the chart is complete. Every slice is proportional to real size, and the total at the centre matches the real amount of space in use.

🌞 Understanding the Sunburst Chart

The sunburst is the heart of petal, and it becomes intuitive after about ten seconds of looking at it.

  • The centre represents the drive or folder you scanned.
  • Each ring outward represents one level deeper into your folder structure.
  • Each slice is a file or folder. Bigger slice, more space.
  • Clicking a slice focuses on that part of the tree so you can keep travelling inward.

This layout lets you answer questions that are painful to answer any other way. Which folder is the giant? Which of its subfolders is the giant? Which file inside that is the giant? With a normal file browser you would click through dozens of folders and sort lists repeatedly. With petal, the answer is already drawn on screen.

Because the chart is live, it updates as the scan progresses. You will see thin slivers of colour gradually fill out, and large blocks appear where big folders are discovered — a very fast way to build confidence that the scan is finding real things rather than spinning its wheels.

💡 Practical Tips for Reclaiming Space

Once you can see exactly what is large, freeing up space becomes a much calmer task. A few reliable starting points:

  • Look for a few huge slices rather than many small ones. Space problems are usually caused by a small handful of large items, not by thousands of tiny files.
  • Check your media folders. Photo libraries, video projects, and audio recordings are the most common space consumers on a modern machine.
  • Look for duplicate archives. Downloads folders often contain the same installer or the same zip downloaded several times over the years.
  • Review old backups and disk images. A single forgotten disk image can quietly hold tens of gigabytes.
  • Re-scan after cleaning. Running petal again confirms the space has genuinely been reclaimed.

petal is a viewer, so it does not delete anything on your behalf. You stay in full control and remove files yourself, in the way you are used to.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to know how to program?

No. petal is a normal desktop application. If you can download a file and open it, you can use petal.

Is petal free?

Yes. petal is an open project and the application is available from the official download page at no cost.

Will petal delete my files?

No. petal only reads and measures. It never removes anything from your drive. Any cleanup is entirely up to you.

Why does the first scan take a while?

The first scan has to walk through every folder on the drive to produce exact numbers. Large drives with hundreds of thousands of files naturally take longer to read than small ones. Because the chart is live, you can start reading it before the scan is complete.

What does "exact" actually mean?

It means the sizes shown are the real sizes of your files, not rounded approximations or sample-based estimates. The totals you see correspond to the space actually occupied on the drive.

Does the sunburst chart update while scanning?

Yes. That is the point of the live chart. It fills in as petal discovers folders, so you get useful information within the first few seconds rather than at the end.

🧩 Technical Details

petal is written in Rust, a language known for speed and memory safety, and it uses GPUI for its interface. It is designed around macOS storage, including APFS volumes, and it reads folders directly from the file system rather than through an abstraction layer.

Project topics include: apfs, disk-space, disk-usage, gpui, macos, rust, storage, sunburst.

If something does not behave as described, the official project page is the place to look for updates, notes, and issue reporting. Checking there first is worthwhile, because fixes and clarifications are published alongside the project itself.
